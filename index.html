Research Experience

Mind Wandering Guidance During Breaks | Graduate Researcher Nov. 2024 – Present
Advisors: Dr. Caitlin Mills & Dr. Aaron Wong, ACC Lab, University of Minnesota Minnesota, USA
  • Innovation: Extended Attention Restoration Theory by examining how minimal guidance ("let your mind wander freely") versus natural mind wandering, combined with physical activity (walking vs. sitting), affects break experiences and restoration following externally-directed (video lectures, Study 2) and internally-directed (argumentative writing, Study 1) cognitive tasks
  • Hypotheses: Predicted that guided mind-wandering would enhance break enjoyment and cognitive restoration (better task performance, reduced post-break mind-wandering) compared to unguided breaks and no-break controls
  • Preliminary Findings (Study 1, N=152): Contrary to predictions, guided instructions decreased new thought frequency (Group × Time: β=−.81, p=.033) and directed to more future plans. Hierarchical piecewise models revealed no restoration benefits, with post-break slopes converging across conditions (all Group × Post-break p>.39), suggesting brief instructions insufficient for cognitive recovery in online contexts
  • Management: Led full project cycle from IRB to implementation, managed data collection and participant compensation, trained research assistants
  • Impact: Presented at CEHD Research Day 2026 and Psychonomic Conference 2025; manuscript for Study 1 in preparation; provides insights for effective break strategies in educational settings
IAMs in Boredom and Depression | Principal Student Researcher Oct. 2023 – May 2024
Advisor: Dr. Yueqin Hu, Health Dynamic Laboratory, Beijing Normal University Beijing, China
  • Innovation: 10-day diary study (N=169, 2,867 memories) examining memory phenomenology and emotion regulation strategies in daily involuntary autobiographical memories (IAMs) between boredom proneness and depression
  • Key Finding: Despite their high correlation (r=.75), multilevel models with both predictors revealed that depression predicted more self-centered (β=.17), rehearsed memories (β=.15), and maladaptive regulation (βs=.25–.34, all p<.05); boredom predicted fewer internal cues (OR=.55, p=.023)
  • Management: Led ethical review application, managed research budget and reimbursement, trained four RAs
  • Impact: Undergraduate Thesis; Presented at APS 2025; provides empirical evidence for boredom-depression distinction
Boredom and Emotional Eating | Principal Student Researcher May 2022 – May 2024
Advisor: Dr. Yueqin Hu, Health Dynamic Laboratory, Beijing Normal University Beijing, China
  • Innovation: Multi-method approach integrating lab experiment (video induction, real-time consumption measurement) with 7-day EMA and Dynamic Structural Equation Modeling to examine bidirectional boredom-eating relationship
  • Key Finding: Lab study: boredom group consumed significantly more chips than sadness/neutral groups (stages 2–3, F(2,139)=7.14, p<.01); EMA: paradoxical dual pathways where snacking directly reduced boredom (β=−.264, p<.01) but increased it indirectly through heightened desire (indirect effect ab=.065, p<.001)
  • Management: Co-authored grant proposal, managed ¥20,000 research budget and expense documentation, coordinated participant recruitment and RA training
  • Impact: Published in Psychological Reports; advances understanding of boredom-snacking regulatory dynamics for healthy eating
